# Kaurivo V4 — source-led brand recognition and sector-specific design

**Universal rule:** A beautiful website in the visual genre of the wrong industry is a design failure. The customer must recognise both the business and its professional context. Logo accuracy alone does not guarantee this.

## Original complaint and observed evidence

Owner feedback: a new bike-related website was perceived as closer to a university than a bicycle service. The repository's Jakomäen suutari ja pyörä concept includes editorial serif headings, muted beige and deep green, institutional-looking strips, a hero dominated by shoemaker imagery, and a strict sequence of brochure sections. This observation is about code and editorial direction, not an independently verified screenshot verdict. The company also provides shoemaker services; neither service may be erased to fix the other.

**Generalise the lesson:** Before drafting, identify the dominant service mix, actual physical environment, brand heritage, price/booking/shop tasks, imagery and the customer's audience. A bicycle repair site should reveal real bikes/workshop/service categories quickly; a cycle retailer needs genuine product browsing if stock is verified; a restaurant needs real food/menu/hours; a salon needs treatments/prices/booking; a builder needs actual projects. Never invent products, stock, equipment, workers or photographs.

## Required review sequence

1. **Original brand fingerprint**: Preserve real identity, typography, colour, original photography, original copy tone, unique parts of the offer, partners, source assets, and the status of media rights. Record both characteristics to retain and characteristics the owner explicitly authorises changing.
2. **Sector fingerprint**: Record the company's customer tasks and at least three independently sourced same-industry operators. Extract what visually distinguishes this industry from universities, agencies, institutions and general templates; do not copy another brand.
3. **Voice fingerprint**: Ground headings in observable product, service, craft or customer language. Avoid repeated slogans, generic mission statements, exaggerated poetic contrasts and identical cross-client page titles.
4. **Composition fingerprint**: Vary hierarchy, storytelling formats, real media treatment, page length and sectional rhythm according to the source material. A huge text banner + split photo + three cards + large CTA strip on every client is a reusable agency template, not custom art direction.
5. **Five-second logo-masked test**: Before showing the logo, ask at least two genuine independent reviewers what type of business and service they think the page represents; check mobile and desktop, first view and all important subpages. False categories (e.g., university instead of bike workshop) are defects. Independently assess what approved original-brand elements they recognise.
6. **Side-by-side owner check**: Compare original/new identity, content, real assets, exact links and customer perception. Preserve the client's voice while improving readability and usability. Redesigning the layout does not authorise changing the business identity.
7. **Publish gate**: The two-reviewer results must be tied to current site/build and original research digests with evidence. An aesthetic score cannot compensate for failed sector recognition or weak brand fidelity.

## Automation boundaries

- Automatically detect when a *verified bicycle business* has no bicycle-related signal in its first main content block.
- Report suspicious repetition of near-identical text-only content strips for visual review, rather than banning useful repeated service cards/catalogues.
- Reject unusually repeated substantial marketing text across unrelated client websites, while allowing common utility headings such as “Yhteystiedot”.
- Keep subjective institutional resemblance and true emotional brand recognition as **REVIEW REQUIRED**, not self-certified machine scores.
- Independently verify that correct original brand assets, authentic photos, copy voice and business tasks are visibly present. Never manufacture a 9.5/10 rating from green code tests.

**Anti-template principle:** One agency quality process, but an original site design language for each client. Avoid generic Kaurivo house-style leaking into customers' identities.
