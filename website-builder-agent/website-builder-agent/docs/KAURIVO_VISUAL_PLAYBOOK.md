# Kaurivo visual playbook

Use the client's verified offer, source identity, approved media and visitor task
to choose a composition. Examples below are reusable principles. Every client
gets an original composition, assets and copy.

## Opening compositions

Choose three concepts with different dominant shapes, image roles, information
order and journeys. Describe alternatives using actual media IDs, a clear
action and a mobile composition. Colour changes alone do not make a concept.

| Composition | Source and execution | Phone adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sculptural centrepiece | Approved product cutout or model dominates an open scene. Place offer and action away from its silhouette. | Use a framed poster/cutout and a compact copy block. Keep action in the natural reading order. |
| Wide cinematic scene | One true project, space or company video fills the field. Align copy with quiet space in the shot. | Art-direct a different crop; make text a real block above or below it when contrast varies. |
| Paired stage and copy | Give image and text unequal mass based on the subject; use a deliberate shared alignment. | Put the offer and action first or immediately after a short image, according to visitor intent. |
| Asymmetric editorial grid | Use one anchor image, one type field and smaller cells only for verified details. Vary scale and alignment. | Stack in task order. Omit decorative cells and avoid broken grids or miniature panels. |
| Masthead with inset project | Give the name or offer a typographic opening, followed by an inset photo with a real project caption. | Rebalance line breaks and image height; maintain a clear next service link. |
| Macro material with context | Use a genuine close-up and one wider image or caption that explains what the visitor is seeing. | Select a crop retaining the texture and context. Put labels outside fine detail. |
| Product or process reveal | Present actual stages of making, use or installation with a visible starting image and a clear path to details. | Use shorter steps or a labelled still gallery with the same information. |
| Project explorer | Show a featured real project and visible routes to others. A spatial gallery is optional when it helps compare. | Present browse controls or a clear project list. Support direct links and keep vertical scrolling. |
| Depth through light and framing | Build depth from the real shot, approved background tones and intentional overlap. A focal glow belongs only to a brand or asset that supports it. | Preserve the subject silhouette and text contrast. Use a stable still. |
| Service-first opening | Lead with the task, region or access details the visitor needs, plus one authentic proof asset. Suitable for practical local services and sparse media. | Expose the call, booking or inquiry without expanding a menu or passing a decorative opener. |
| Space or live model | Show an approved real model with an inspection purpose, such as viewing a form or navigating a space. Load the viewer after the initial offer and poster. | Offer preview photos and tap controls. Preserve the offer and navigation if WebGL is unavailable. |

## Colour and backgrounds

- Define roles from verified brand identity: base canvas, secondary surface,
  primary ink, secondary ink, divider, brand accent, actionable fill and focus.
  Keep the approved mark recognisable on each surface. Document the source for
  brand colours and derive supporting tints or shades conservatively.
- Establish hierarchy with lightness and area first, then hue and saturation.
  Give the main action a predictable colour role. Reuse accent sparingly for
  orientation and interaction. Build distinct selected, focus and error states;
  use labels or shape in addition to colour where the state has meaning.
- Use warm/cool temperature and restrained complements only when supported by
  the original palette or actual photography. Don't pick a new brand from a
  reference site's palette or an incidental photo colour.
- Choose the background for its job: a quiet solid surface for dense reading;
  an approved material close-up for tactile identity; a bounded photo field for
  real context; an intentional tonal gradient to improve separation or express
  approved brand lighting. A global noise filter, spotlight, generic mesh or
  dark band behind every section weakens legibility and identity.
- Over media, inspect actual contrast across the full text area, phone crops
  and video poster/playback states. Reposition the copy or give it a stable
  surface if the image lightness shifts. Check links, focus, form controls and
  identity on each surface. Do not recolour evidence photos to fit the palette.
- Use type scale, line length, alignment and image rhythm to change pace across
  sections. Choose fonts for the brand and its languages, including Finnish
  and Swedish characters. Use meaningful headings and text without repeated
  decorative eyebrows. Keep real imagery and copy visible on first render.

## Page roles and handoff from the hero

| Page | First screen and next content |
| --- | --- |
| Home | Verified offer, strongest authentic proof and one main action. Follow with a real service decision or project, using a compatible visual rhythm. |
| Service index | Put the actual service categories or tasks first. Differentiate them with concrete descriptions and relevant imagery where available. |
| Service detail | Lead with the customer problem and scope. Show what is included, a verified example or process, then the route to a relevant inquiry. Use facts the company can substantiate. |
| Project or gallery | Show the real outcome and context. Add materials, constraints and before/after only when verified. Link to the relevant service. |
| Product or menu | Expose actual products/items, essential properties and verified prices or allergens where relevant. Preserve real ordering or checkout. |
| Contact or booking | Put real channels, hours and the actual action up front. Verify inputs, validation, success state and destination. |
| Pricing | Make real prices, scope and package differences legible. Keep qualifying details close to their price and next action. |

Place genuine ratings and proof beside a decision they support. Use the current
logo and a readable header. Keep route-specific imagery and openings coherent
through typography, surfaces and navigation without duplicating the home layout.

## Motion and interaction choices

- **Transition as scroll**. Use one section-level handoff to reveal a detail or
  connect two steps of a real story. Prefer an unobstructed natural scroll and
  a progress-independent reading order. Provide complete still content.
- **Sequence or before/after**. Use true related frames for actual stages. Use
  explicit controls and source labels for before and after. Verify forward and
  backward use, touch input and reduced-motion presentation.
- **Live 3D**. Use an approved model with an inspection purpose and a normal
  static poster. Preserve controls, semantic content and a usable route if the
  asset or renderer is absent. Load heavy assets only when useful.
- **Spatial browsing**. Use direct project or product controls and a list
  alternative. Maintain visible selection, keyboard navigation and click/tap
  targets. Do not let horizontal gestures trap vertical page scrolling.
- **Touchable surface**. Use tactile depth where it clarifies an interactive
  object. Provide equivalent tap and keyboard behaviour for information or
  actions. Avoid decorative cursor lighting and full-page movement.
- **Subtle transitions**. Use transform/opacity for non-essential feedback,
  with a stable reduced-motion state. Render the offer, contact and main action
  immediately. Regular scrolling and navigation remain available.
